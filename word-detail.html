你好 nǐ hǎo • Hello / Hi
Home / HSK Library / HSK 1 / 你好

📝 Key Points

  • Most common greeting in Chinese
  • Used at any time of day (unlike "good morning")
  • Can be used both formally and informally
  • Literally means "you good"

💬 Example Sentences

1

你好！很高兴认识你。

Nǐ hǎo! Hěn gāoxìng rènshi nǐ.

Hello! Nice to meet you.

2

你好，请问洗手间在哪里？

Nǐ hǎo, qǐngwèn xǐshǒujiān zài nǎlǐ?

Hello, where is the restroom?

3

老师好！

Lǎoshī hǎo!

Hello teacher!

💡 When greeting teachers or elders, use their title instead of "你"

⚠️ Common Mistakes

❌

你好吗？(as a greeting)

✓

你好！

"你好吗？" means "How are you?" and requires an answer. For a simple greeting, use "你好！"

❌

Nǐhǎo (wrong tone)

✓

Nǐ hǎo (3rd tone + 3rd tone)

Pay attention to tones! Both characters use the 3rd tone (falling-rising).

Etymology and Structure

你好 (nǐ hǎo) is composed of two characters:

  • 你 (nǐ) - "you" (second person pronoun)
  • 好 (hǎo) - "good" (adjective)

Literally translated, it means "you good?" but is used as a greeting equivalent to "hello" in English. This structure reflects a cultural emphasis on checking on someone's wellbeing as part of a greeting.

Historical Context

The greeting "你好" became standardized in modern Chinese (白话文) during the early 20th century. Before this, classical Chinese used different greeting forms that varied by social status and context...

🏮 Cultural Insight: In traditional Chinese culture, greetings often involved询问 about one's recent activities or health, reflecting a community-oriented mindset.

Tone and Pronunciation

你 nǐ (3rd tone)

Falling-rising tone

好 hǎo (3rd tone)

Falling-rising tone

💡 When two 3rd tones appear together, the first one often changes to a 2nd tone sound (though written as 3rd). So "nǐ hǎo" sounds like "ní hǎo" in natural speech.
